Work in Progress

Shadow Credit and Firm Performance

I investigate how reliance on shadow, non-bank lenders shapes firm outcomes after borrowing. Using the 2013-14 leveraged-lending guidance as a plausibly exogenous shift that constrained banks but not nonbanks, I plan to assemble a loan-firm panel and compare firms that reallocate toward nonbanks with matched bank-financed peers.

Publications and Working Papers

Connectedness of G10 Cross-Currency Basis Derivatives Markets With Komla Agudze, Favour Olarewaju, and Chikaodinaka Iwuagwu. The Journal of Investing, 34(1), 58-82, 2024.

We examine interconnectedness across G10 cross-currency basis swap markets using the Diebold-Yilmaz framework to identify shock transmitters and recipients. The EUR/USD basis emerges as the dominant net giver and least recipient of shocks, making the euro basis an important market to track for FX hedging.

Dynamic Spillovers Among Dirty Energy Market and Stocks of Leading Emitters of Carbon With Johnson A. Oliyide, Sodiq A. Oladipupo, Oluwafemi David Ogunjemilua, and Fisayo Ajayi. Working paper, 2024.

Applying quantile time-frequency connectedness methods, we study spillovers among crude oil, natural gas, and equities in the United States, China, Japan, and India. The results show that short-term, tail-state risks dominate and that spillover roles shift across quantiles, frequencies, and crisis periods.

A New Testable Theory on Deviations from Covered Interest Rate Parity Working paper, 2022. Paper

I develop an approximate closed-form model linking covered-interest-parity deviations, measured by the cross-currency basis, to credit-spread differentials, technology/output differences, and inflation gaps. A simple empirical exercise supports the theory and highlights implications for fixed-income and FX-derivatives arbitrage.

Extended Market Drivers of CIP Deviations in Korea's Cross-Currency Swap Markets With F.O. Olarewaju. Asian Review of Financial Research, 33(4), 541-580, 2020. Paper

We study the one- and five-year Korean won cross-currency basis across pre-GFC, GFC, eurozone crisis, and post-crisis periods. Regression models with ARMA errors and VAR variance decompositions show that CDS, stock returns, and Libor-OIS were the strongest GFC drivers.
